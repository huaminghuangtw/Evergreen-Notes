A belief is an interpretation of reality, not reality itself. Yet we rarely act that way. We treat our beliefs as facts, then live—and suffer—as if the interpretation were true.

# The Method

Belief Analysis is a structured four-step inquiry for examining a belief that is causing you pain.

The original four steps, preserved verbatim:

1. **Verify the truth.** Ask yourself: is it really true? Like, is it _really_ true? Have you really got incontrovertible evidence to support this? Could there be no other way of interpreting the data other than the belief?
2. **Feel the consequence.** How does this belief make you feel? What thoughts or behaviors does it lead to?
3. **Imagine its absence.** Imagine a world in which this belief didn’t exist in your mind. You’ve never even entertained the thought of the belief. How do you feel in this situation? What thoughts or behaviors does this new state lead to?
4. **Test the opposite.** Consider the _opposite_ of the belief. Come up with 3 reasons why the _opposite_ is true.
	* “Acting as if…”
	* [Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy](rational-emotive-behavior-therapy.md)

# Example

Take the belief: **“I’m not good enough at my work.”**

1. **Is it really true?** What is the incontrovertible evidence? You got the job, you’ve shipped projects, you’ve received positive feedback. The missed deadline can be read as “I’m incompetent” or as “the scope was too big and I overcommitted.” There is no version of the data that proves, beyond doubt, that you are not good enough.
2. **How does it make you feel?** Anxious, small, defensive. It leads you to overwork to compensate, to avoid asking for help for fear of being exposed, and to hold back ideas in meetings—classic [impostor syndrome](impostor-syndrome.md).
3. **Without the belief?** Same track record, but you would feel calmer. You would ask questions, take on stretch assignments, and treat feedback as data rather than as a verdict on your worth. The situation is identical; only the story changed.
4. **The opposite: “I am good enough.”** Three reasons: (a) you were given the responsibility in the first place; (b) people keep coming to you for input; (c) you have measurably improved over time. **Acting as if:** speak up in the next meeting, volunteer for the hard problem, ask for the raise—before you feel ready. The action leads, the feeling follows.
