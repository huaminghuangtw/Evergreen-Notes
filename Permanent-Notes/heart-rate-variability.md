**Heart Rate Variability (HRV, 心率變異度)** is the variation in the time [^1] between consecutive heartbeats — not how _fast_ your heart beats, but how _unevenly_ it beats.

HRV is a biomarker of resilience and adaptability.

# Your heart is not a metronome

Suppose your heart beats 60 times per minute. You might imagine:

> 1 second → ❤️ → 1 second → ❤️ → 1 second → ❤️

But your actual heart might do:

> 0.92s → ❤️ → 1.05s → ❤️ → 0.98s → ❤️ → 1.08s → ❤️

The **variation in the time between each heartbeat** is HRV.

And here’s the surprising part: **a healthy heart doesn’t beat perfectly regularly.** Your nervous system is constantly adjusting your heart based on breathing, movement, stress, sleep, digestion, etc.

You can feel this yourself: when you **inhale**, your heart speeds up slightly; when you **exhale**, it slows down.

Imagine two people whose average heart rate is exactly **60 bpm**:

**Person A:**

> 1.00s → 1.00s → 1.00s → 1.00s

**Person B:**

> 0.90s → 1.10s → 0.95s → 1.05s

Both average 60 bpm, but Person B’s heart rhythm is responding more dynamically.

That’s why HRV can be a **proxy for autonomic flexibility** — how much room the system has to shift gears instead of being stuck in one mode.

# Think of your heart as a car

* **Sympathetic nervous system** = accelerator
* **Parasympathetic nervous system** = brake
* **Autonomic flexibility** = how smoothly you can use both

A healthy nervous system doesn’t tell the heart, _“Stay at exactly 60 bpm.”_ It constantly makes tiny adjustments based on what you’re doing. Generally, **higher HRV can indicate greater autonomic flexibility**.

# What tends to raise HRV

* **Sleep** — the single biggest lever, especially consistent sleep—wake times
* **Regular aerobic exercise** (endurance athletes typically have high HRV)
* **Slow, paced breathing** at ~6 breaths per minute ([Coherent Breathing](breathing-techniques.md)) — it maximizes beat-to-beat swings and trains the baroreflex
* **Meditation** and other relaxation practices

# How to read your own numbers

* **Watch the trend, not the reading.** One low morning is noise; a week-long drift is a signal.

	> **Short readings** (1–5 min, e.g. a morning spot check) are sensitive to breathing, posture, and movement; **24-hour readings** average all of that out.

* **Higher isn’t automatically better.** Heart rhythm disorders and acute illness can also inflate the number.
* HRV is a **proxy, not a verdict** — one input among many, not a scoreboard for your life.

[^1]: HRV is expressed in **milliseconds** (the gap between beats), never in beats per minute.
