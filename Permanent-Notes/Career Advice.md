# Two Types of Opportunities

* “Glass Doors”
	* Opportunities where you can see exactly what’s there
	* It’s obvious win, with a tangible payoff
* “Wooden Doors”
	* Opportunities where you can’t see what’s on the other side until you put in the effort to knock and go through them
	* It’s opaque upfront, with only the potential for an outsized payoff

# Networking is dead

You don’t get anywhere by accumulating thousands of transactional connections. You get somewhere by building genuine relationships.

* Acting in the service of others
* Creating value for those around you
* Giving with no intention of receiving in return

 **[Stop networking, start building.](https://www.sahilbloom.com/newsletter/the-anti-networking-guide)**

Networking is saying “I’m friends with you to hopefully leverage your influence for my personal benefit.” Friendship is saying “I want more for you than from you. Seeing you win makes me happy.” **[Friendship is the way.](a-friend-to-all-is-a-friend-to-none.md)**

Networking is not about connecting yourself to others, but connecting other people to other people.

When you meet someone that you want a relationship with, you gotta think how can I add value to their life. Not vice versa

# 學習「向上管理」，老闆開心、滿意最重要

* Good upper management avoids surprises and keeps managers in the loop on what we are doing. The amount of context we think we are over-communicating is usually the right amount.

---

Always communicate more than feels necessary. Literally ask questions until someone tells you that you’re asking to many questions.

* Ask for early code reviews
* Start design discussions early
* Announce releases and milestones visible to the team
* Raise potential risks and failure modes before they become incidents

Over-communication prevents surprises, aligns expectations, and builds trust.

When in doubt, communicate.

# Always be prepared to leave your employer because they are prepared to leave you.

> “Your best job will be one that you were unqualified for because it stretches you. In fact, only apply to jobs you are unqualified for.” — Kevin Kelly, [Excellent Advice for Living: Wisdom I Wish I’d Known Earlier](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/98113859)

> “When you are looking for a job, remember that somewhere an employer is desperately hunting for someone like you especially if you are unconventional. Your real job is to make that match happen and it is worth whatever time it takes.” — Kevin Kelly, [Excellent Advice for Living: Wisdom I Wish I’d Known Earlier](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/98113859)

Regularly/consistently apply to jobs while you’re already employed. It’s the best time to find a new job, as you can negotiate way harder if you don’t have the pressure of needing a job. Always be looking. Don’t wait until you are desperate for a new job.

* Worst case: continue to be employed in the current role
* Best case: get a pay bump in a new role

Wrong mindset: “I have a job so I don’t have to look for a new job.”

# The WTF Notebook

A “[WTF Notebook](https://www.simplermachines.com/why-you-need-a-wtf-notebook/)” is a valuable tool for capturing immediate problems or oddities you notice at work. <mark>Instead of acting impulsively or sharing every concern with your team right away, you write down anything that seems strange or problematic.</mark> This practice helps you pause, gather more context, and prioritize what to address later, rather than feeling compelled to fix everything as soon as you notice it.

# 如何成為一位好的面試官：用「閒聊」看見真實的內在

> 好的面試官，不是找最會回答的人，而是讓對方最能展現自己的人。

## 為什麼要閒聊？

刻意設計的面試題目，往往只測得到「準備能力」；

而自然的閒聊，反而能挖掘一個人隱而不顯的內在偏好與真實性格。

這類問題有三個特點：

1. **不容易事前準備**
2. **即興編造的成本很高**
3. **只要追問細節，真假很容易被拆穿**

因此，即使對方試圖「瞎掰」，在具體描述與連續追問下，往往會露出破綻/馬腳。

## 從「閒暇時間」可以看出一個人的性格

一個人如何使用不被約束的時間，往往比工作表現更誠實地反映他的價值觀與動機。

* 「在假日或閒暇時，你電腦的瀏覽器通常會開哪些分頁？」
* 「最近一次你完全放鬆的一天，是怎麼過的？」
* 「如果明天突然多出一個完全沒安排的下午，你會選擇做什麼？」

> 👉 觀察重點：是否偏向探索、學習、創作、社交，或單純消耗娛樂？

## 從「興趣嗜好」可以看出內在驅動力

* 「你最近一次為了興趣，主動花時間深入研究的是什麼？」
* 「這個興趣你大概維持多久了？有沒有某個轉折點？」
* 「你有沒有哪個興趣，是你即使沒人要求、也會自己一直做的？」

> 👉 觀察重點：持續性、好奇心深度、是否享受過程而非成果。

## 從「工作之外的時間安排」，看出自我管理與能量來源

人如何分配非工作時間，往往反映他的界線感、節奏感與自我覺察能力。

* 「下班後你通常會怎麼讓自己『切換狀態』？」
* 「最近一次覺得自己快要累壞，是怎麼調整回來的？」
* 「你覺得什麼樣的生活節奏，對你來說是最舒服的？」

> 👉 觀察重點：是否有自我照顧意識、是否知道自己何時該加速或停下。

## 進階追問技巧：讓真實自然浮現

問得深 > 問得多

* **時間軸追問**：「那是什麼時候開始的？」
* **細節追問**：「你剛剛提到的那個情境，可以再具體一點嗎？」
* **對照追問**：「那跟你現在有什麼不一樣？」
