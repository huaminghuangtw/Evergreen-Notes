**Thinking frameworks (思考框架)** are 2×2 grids: name two axes, and each cell tells you what to do with whatever lands in it. The grid is never the point — naming the two axes *before* you decide is.

Collected from [Foursight](https://foursight.ginatrapani.org) by Gina Trapani. Every grid there is an editable canvas with a shareable link; tap a quadrant to rewrite it.

# Choose What to Work On

## Eisenhower Matrix

> Decide what to do now vs. later. Vertical = importance, horizontal = urgency.

|  | **URGENT** | **NOT URGENT** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IMPORTANT** | **Do first** — time-sensitive and adding value | **Schedule** — important but not time-sensitive; put it on your calendar |
| **NOT IMPORTANT** | **Delegate** — time-sensitive, but doesn't move your goals | **Don't do** — neither time-sensitive nor important |

Source: [Eisenhower.me](https://www.eisenhower.me/eisenhower-matrix/)

## Impact/Effort Matrix

> Choose which initiatives to start. Vertical = impact, horizontal = effort.

|  | **LOW EFFORT** | **HIGH EFFORT** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HIGH IMPACT** | **Quick wins** — big return, little risk; do these first | **Big bets** — transformative but risky and resource-hungry; plan before committing |
| **LOW IMPACT** | **Fill-ins** — low value, low effort; use blocked time, don't let them expand | **Money pit** — high effort, no return; spot it early and redirect the resources |

Source: [Product School](https://productschool.com/blog/product-fundamentals/impact-effort-matrix)

## Risk/Reward Quadrants

> Decide what to bet on. Vertical = risk, horizontal = reward.

|  | **LOW REWARD** | **HIGH REWARD** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LOW RISK** | **Boring** — cheap to hold, no upside | **Smart** — upside without the downside; take these |
| **HIGH RISK** | **Stupid** — cost without upside; refuse | **Gambling** — real upside, real chance of ruin; size the bet so you can lose it |

Source: [Dividend Strategy](https://dividendstrategy.ca/risk-adjusted-returns/risk-reward-quadrants/)

# Size Up the Situation

## SWOT Analysis

> Audit a position before acting on it. Vertical = internal vs. external, horizontal = helpful vs. harmful.

|  | **HELPFUL** | **HARMFUL** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **INTERNAL** | **Strengths** — advantages, what you do better than anyone, unique resources | **Weaknesses** — what to improve, where you're out-resourced, what others see as weak |
| **EXTERNAL** | **Opportunities** — openings you can spot, trends to ride, strengths you can convert | **Threats** — what could harm you, what competitors are doing, exposures your weaknesses create |

Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWOT_analysis)

## Growth Share Matrix

> Allocate resources across products, services, or lines of business. Vertical = market growth, horizontal = market share.

|  | **HIGH MARKET SHARE** | **LOW MARKET SHARE** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HIGH GROWTH** | **Stars** — leaders; generate cash but need ongoing investment to hold position | **Question marks** — need heavy investment to win share and become stars |
| **LOW GROWTH** | **Cash cows** — mature, reliable cash flow, little investment needed | **Pets** — keep resources minimal, or divest unless they serve another goal |

Source: [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/about/overview/our-history/growth-share-matrix)

## Knowns and Unknowns

> Map what you actually know before planning. Vertical = understanding, horizontal = awareness.

|  | **AWARE** | **NOT AWARE** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UNDERSTAND** | **Known knowns** — you know it and you get it | **Unknown knowns** — you understand it but aren't aware you do: habit, intuition, tacit skill |
| **DON'T UNDERSTAND** | **Known unknowns** — you know a gap exists; go research it | **Unknown unknowns** — neither aware nor understood; where planning actually fails |

Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_are_unknown_unknowns)

# Lead Other People

## Skill/Will Matrix

> Pick a management move for one person. Vertical = will, horizontal = skill.

|  | **LOW SKILL** | **HIGH SKILL** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HIGH WILL** | **Guide** *(contributors)* — training and detailed instructions | **Challenge** *(high performers)* — delegate and give autonomy to decide |
| **LOW WILL** | **Direct** *(low performers)* — close supervision and clear direction | **Motivate** *(potential detractors)* — find what demotivates them and re-engage |

Source: [Stratechi](https://www.stratechi.com/will-skill-matrix/)

## Management Styles

> Read a manager's posture. Vertical = concern for people, horizontal = concern for results.

|  | **LOW RESULTS CONCERN** | **HIGH RESULTS CONCERN** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HIGH PEOPLE CONCERN** | **Country club** — friendly and easy tempo, sometimes at the cost of performance; low turnover | **Integrative** — democratic; results without writing people off; long-term trust and development |
| **LOW PEOPLE CONCERN** | **Impoverished** — disengaged, laissez-faire; doing just enough not to get fired | **Authoritarian** — "produce or perish"; people as commodities; high turnover |

Source: [Leading Sapiens](https://www.leadingsapiens.com/blake-mouton-leadership-grid-styles/)

# See Yourself Clearly

## Johari Window

> Understand the gap between how you see yourself and how others see you. Vertical = known to others, horizontal = known to yourself.

|  | **KNOWN TO SELF** | **UNKNOWN TO SELF** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **KNOWN TO OTHERS** | **Open self** — both of you know it; the basis of trust | **Blind self** — they see it, you don't; feedback closes the gap |
| **UNKNOWN TO OTHERS** | **Hidden self** — you know it, they don't; disclosure closes the gap | **Unknown self** — nobody knows yet; shows up under new conditions |

Source: [TSW Training](https://www.tsw.co.uk/blog/leadership-and-management/the-johari-window/)

## Disapproval Matrix

> Decide whose criticism deserves attention. Vertical = rational vs. irrational, horizontal = whether they know you.

|  | **KNOW YOU** | **DON'T KNOW YOU** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RATIONAL** | **Lovers** — want you to improve and thrive; listen hardest to these | **Critics** — experts in your field, criticizing the work and not you; weigh it |
| **IRRATIONAL** | **Frenemies** — don't want you to improve: exes, toddlers, undermining coworkers; discount | **Haters** — YouTube commenters, jealous ignoramuses; ignore |

Source: [Ann Friedman](https://www.annfriedman.com/post/49152967734/in-my-ongoing-quest-for-the-perfect-framework-for)

## Creativity Types

> Recognize which mode of creativity you're in. Vertical = deliberate vs. spontaneous, horizontal = cognitive vs. emotional.

|  | **COGNITIVE** | **EMOTIONAL** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DELIBERATE** | **Deliberate cognitive** — purposeful: talent plus deep knowledge of one area, grinding toward a planned goal | **Deliberate emotional** — reflective: sensitive, private, quiet time; the diary-keeping type |
| **SPONTANEOUS** | **Spontaneous cognitive** — the "Aha!" in bed or in the shower; unconscious mind takes over | **Spontaneous emotional** — the epiphany type; most outstanding artists — musicians, painters, writers |

Source: [Entrepreneur's Data](https://www.entrepreneursdata.com/types-of-creativity-in-entrepreneurship-fully-explained/) on Arne Dietrich's four types
