# 唯心主義

**Idealism (literally “mind-only-ism”)** is the position that mind, consciousness, or ideas are fundamental, and that matter is either a construction of minds or an expression of a mind-like principle.

## 主要流派 (Variants)

* **主觀唯心論 (Subjective idealism)** — George Berkeley: 存在即被感知 (_esse est percipi_). Objects have no existence apart from a perceiving mind; the world stays stable and shared only because it is continuously perceived by God.
* **客觀唯心論 (Objective idealism)** — Plato’s 理型 (Forms), Hegel’s 絕對精神 (Absolute Spirit / Geist), 朱熹的「理」: the order of the world is itself a kind of Idea, independent of any individual mind.
* **先驗唯心論 (Transcendental idealism)** — Kant: we can never know 物自身 (the thing-in-itself, _Ding an sich_); experience is always already shaped by the mind’s own forms — space, time, causality. In effect: 我們看到的不是世界本身，而是世界配上我們的認知介面。See [The Map Is Not the Territory](the-map-is-not-the-territory.md).
* **唯識 (Yogācāra, “consciousness-only”)** — 萬法唯識: experience is the ripening of 種子 (karmic seeds) stored in 阿賴耶識 (ālaya-vijñāna, the storehouse consciousness).
* **泛心論 (Panpsychism)** — the modern cousin: consciousness is a basic feature of matter everywhere, like mass or charge; complex minds are combinations of simpler ones.

# 唯物主義

**Materialism / Physicalism** is the position that matter and energy (物質) are fundamental, and that mind, consciousness and ideas are _products_ of physical processes rather than their source.

# 常見誤解 (Common Confusions)

* **唯心主義 ≠ 理想主義** The Chinese 唯心 sounds like 理想, but it is not “having ideals” or 「想太多」 — it is a claim about what reality is made of.
* **唯物主義 ≠ 物慾主義／拜金** Materialism as metaphysics is not 「愛錢」 or 「只在乎物質享受」 — that is consumerism. Marx’s materialism is in fact one of the sharpest critiques of money-worship.
* **唯心主義 ≠ 否認物質世界存在** Most idealists do not deny that chairs are solid; they argue that the chair’s solidity is mind-dependent, not mind-independent.
* **唯物主義 ≠ 否認意識存在** Only the eliminativist fringe dismisses experience itself; most physicalists take consciousness to be real — just not fundamental.
